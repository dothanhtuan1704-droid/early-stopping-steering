\begin{abstract}
Small Language Models (SLMs) offer practical utility for clinical guidance in low-resource settings, but frequently exhibit domain-specific hallucinations in specialized languages like Vietnamese. We propose Early-Stopping Activation Steering, an inference-time intervention evaluating temporally bounded vector injection at Layer~8 of Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct under Hard Cutoff and Linear Decay schedules ($K{=}16$, decoding steps $t \in [1, 16]$). Under natural completion ($T=800$), Hard Cutoff ($K{=}16$) achieves 70.60\% automated reference-preference accuracy (+5.20~pp over baseline 65.40\%, item-level exact McNemar $p=0.00086$, Holm $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0026$) with 100\% EOS hit rate; however, longer outputs coincide with lower token-level BERTScore F1 (0.6695 vs 0.6779) and higher 4-gram repetition (5.35\% vs 4.10\%), a divergence whose causal driver remains unestablished. Under bounded stress testing ($T=200$), Linear Decay ($K{=}16$) achieves 77.40\% RefPref (+4.20~pp over baseline 73.20\%, item-level exact McNemar $p=0.0055$, Holm $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0165$, 95\% CI $[+1.40, +7.00]$~pp), showing no statistically significant separation from multi-retriever Hybrid RRF RAG (76.20\%, $p=0.4709$) and exceeding lexical BM25 RAG (+8.80~pp, $p=3.85 \times 10^{-7}$) without external retrieval overhead. An exploratory pipeline combining Hybrid RAG with post-hoc selected Hard Cutoff yields 80.40\% RefPref (+1.00~pp over RAG alone, $p=0.3018$, non-significant marginal gain; 4.46\% Rep-4 vs 4.63\%). Directional controls ($N{=}100$ isotropic vectors, $55.82\% \pm 4.15\%$ sample SD) establish a standardized descriptive separation of $z_{\text{sep}}=5.20\sigma$ over isotropic noise. While early-stopping steering provides a lightweight, retrieval-free preference alignment mechanism, evaluations reflect the joint effect of schedule and cumulative dose within a single model family, warranting cross-architecture and clinical validation.

\keywords{Activation steering \and Factual preference alignment \and Clinical language models \and Vietnamese NLP \and Inference-time intervention.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across healthcare applications \cite{ref3}. However, in high-stakes domains requiring rigorous factual precision---such as clinical pharmacology and prescription guidance in non-English languages---standard models suffer from domain-specific hallucinations \cite{ref7}. In Vietnamese clinical contexts, these errors range from subtle phrasing inaccuracies to hazardous contradictions, such as recommending contraindicated medications during pregnancy or miscalculating pediatric dosages.

Standard mitigation strategies include supervised fine-tuning (SFT) \cite{ref10} and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) \cite{ref5}. Although RAG supplies external context passages, models may fail to attend to retrieved evidence when internal activation pathways favor non-truthful generation \cite{ref11}, while incurring indexing overheads and context window expansion. Supervised fine-tuning demands substantial annotated resources and risks catastrophic forgetting of general linguistic capabilities \cite{ref12}.

Recently, Representation Engineering (RepE) \cite{ref1} and Inference-Time Intervention (ITI) \cite{ref2} have introduced lightweight, non-destructive alternatives. By extracting a contrastive steering vector $v_{\text{steer}}$ from hidden activations and injecting it into intermediate layers during autoregressive generation, activation steering guides completions toward reference-aligned outputs without modifying model weights. Related paradigms including Activation Addition \cite{ref8} and contrastive activation addition \cite{ref9} have demonstrated linear steerability for behavioral alignment.

However, continuous activation steering ($K=\infty$) induces persistent hidden-state norm displacement over long sequences. In this work, we present \textbf{Early-Stopping Activation Steering}, an inference-time framework evaluating temporally bounded intervention via Hard Cutoff and Linear Decay schedules restricting vector injection to the first $K=16$ decoding steps ($t \in [1, 16]$, influencing tokens $y_2 \ldots y_{17}$). We benchmark finite schedules against continuous intervention under matched initial scale $\alpha_0=18.0$, while analyzing the confounding role of cumulative steering dose.

Our core contributions are: (i)~\textbf{ViHaluEval-Medical Benchmark:} A curated benchmark of 14,674 core clinical pairs (screened via automated consistency rules from 14,700 candidates) derived from the official Vietnamese National Drug Formulary (2nd Edition, 2018) \cite{ref6}, featuring a primary-API partition disjoint test split ($N_{\text{test}}=500$) and a 50-sample human spot-check audit (94.0\% alignment, 95\% Clopper-Pearson CI $[83.5\%, 98.7\%]$). (ii)~\textbf{Dual-Horizon Evaluation Protocol:} Systematic evaluation under natural completion ($T=800$) and stress testing ($T=200$), supported by formulary audit logs and automated quality filtering metrics. (iii)~\textbf{Activation-Norm Dynamics:} Tracking Layer~8 activation norms across $N=50$ prompts, documenting that direct vector displacement ceases post-cutoff ($\Delta_{\text{norm}}=0.00$ by construction) and that observed differences in post-cutoff mean pre-hook scalar norms relative to baseline are not statistically significant ($p=0.1050$, failing to reject equal mean scalar magnitudes without proving latent trajectory equivalence). (iv)~\textbf{Directional Selectivity \& Subspace Controls:} Negative steering (62.80\%), a single label-permutation split (68.60\%), $N=20$ covariance-matched subspace vectors ($74.81\% \pm 0.95\%$), and $N=100$ isotropic random vectors ($55.82\% \pm 4.15\%$), establishing a descriptive $z_{\text{sep}}=5.20\sigma$ separation over isotropic noise while providing bounded initial comparisons against unoriented covariance perturbation and label permutation. (v)~\textbf{Statistical Validation \& Ablation:} Item-level paired $2\times 2$ binomial McNemar testing ($p=0.0055$ at $T=200$; $p=0.00086$ at $T=800$) and strength ablation ($\alpha_0 \in \{15.0, 18.0, 20.0\}$).

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Hallucination in Clinical LMs.} LLM hallucinations in clinical settings present severe patient safety risks \cite{ref7}. Singhal et al.~\cite{ref3} demonstrated that while large models encode extensive medical knowledge, they remain susceptible to factual errors in contraindications and posology. Benchmarks such as HaluEval \cite{ref13} and TruthfulQA \cite{ref16} established foundational evaluation frameworks that informed our domain-specific ViHaluEval-Medical benchmark.

\textbf{Inference-Time Intervention \& Representation Engineering.} Rather than updating weights through parameter fine-tuning \cite{ref10}, inference-time methods intervene directly in intermediate representations. Li et al.~\cite{ref2} proposed ITI, identifying truthful attention heads. Zou et al.~\cite{ref1} generalized this into Representation Engineering (RepE). Turner et al.~\cite{ref8} introduced ActAdd, and Rimsky et al.~\cite{ref9} developed contrastive activation addition. Our study investigates temporal intervention dynamics by comparing Hard Cutoff and Linear Decay schedules against continuous injection.

\textbf{Evaluation Metrics for Clinical Text.} Traditional n-gram overlap metrics like ROUGE \cite{ref14} often fail to assess semantic fidelity in non-English clinical text. Zhang et al.~\cite{ref15} introduced BERTScore, calculating token-level contextual cosine similarities. We employ a BERTScore-based reference-preference criterion as an automated semantic preference proxy.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{ViHaluEval-Medical Benchmark Construction}\label{sec:benchmark}
The ViHaluEval-Medical benchmark comprises 14,674 curated, rule-filtered Vietnamese clinical Q\&A pairs derived from 14,576 unique text passages of the official Vietnamese National Drug Formulary (\vn{Dược thư Quốc gia Việt Nam}, 2nd Edition, 2018) \cite{ref6}, covering 1,026 comprehensive drug monographs. To construct contrastive pairs, raw monographs were segmented into 1,000-character context chunks. A high-throughput vLLM engine powered by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ (batch size 64, sampling temperature $\tau = 0.7$, top-$p = 0.9$, max tokens $= 650$) executed structured prompts on each chunk to generate clinical questions ($q_i$), each paired with a formulary-grounded reference answer ($y_i^+$) and a counter-answer ($y_i^-$). We note that using Qwen2.5-AWQ to synthesize benchmark pairs introduces potential same-family stylistic affinities with the evaluated model. The core benchmark spans three clinical categories: Special Dosage Modifications ($N=5,000$, 34.07\%), Contradictory Pregnancy Safety ($N=4,885$, 33.29\%), and Misleading Drug Interactions ($N=4,789$, 32.64\%), strictly summing to $N=14,674$ core pairs.

\textbf{Automated Verification \& Quality Filtering Protocol.} All raw generated candidate triplets ($N=14,700$ raw pairs) underwent automated rule-based cross-checking combining regex entity parsing with key-phrase monograph matching. Criteria required: (i)~reference answers $y^+$ to demonstrate strict monograph concordance without numerical conflict, and (ii)~counter-answers $y^-$ to exhibit unambiguous domain contradictions: (a)~altering pediatric or renal dosage parameters by scalar multipliers of $2\times$ to $5\times$, (b)~inverting pregnancy safety classifications (e.g., reclassifying ``contraindicated'' as ``safe under supervision''), or (c)~flipping drug-drug interaction polarities. Candidate pairs failing automated checks, exhibiting ambiguous contradictions, or falling into non-core categories were discarded ($N=26$ non-conforming items removed: 14 regex/formatting errors, 8 duplicate prompts, 4 font encoding artifacts, retaining $N=14,674$ core pairs). For combination drugs, items were deterministically assigned to the primary active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) to enforce primary-API partition disjointness across dataset splits.

\textbf{Human Audit \& Dataset Partitioning.} A manual audit across a stratified sample of $N=50$ test questions evaluated monograph grounding directly against official formulary entries, confirming 94.0\% (47/50, 95\% Clopper-Pearson exact CI $[83.5\%, 98.7\%]$) strict monograph alignment, with 3/50 non-alignments attributed to dosage snippet truncation or administration route ambiguity. Benchmark data is partitioned at the API level into a development split ($N_{\text{dev}}=12,495$ pairs, 85.15\%: training $N_{\text{train}}=10,272$ for vector estimation; validation $N_{\text{val}}=2,223$ for hyperparameter tuning) and a disjoint held-out test partition ($N_{\text{test\_partition}}=2,179$ pairs, 14.85\%), strictly summing to $N=14,674$ total records. From the test partition, an evaluation sample of $N_{\text{test}}=500$ questions was drawn using stratified random sampling with fixed seed 42: Pregnancy Safety ($N=165$), Drug Interactions ($N=160$), and Special Dosage ($N=175$). We emphasize that while primary statistical tests treat test questions as item-level observations, items originating from the same API or monograph share latent contextual information, an issue we analyze in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\subsection{Contrastive Vector Estimation \& Directional Controls}\label{sec:vector}
Given prompt $x_i$, reference answer $y_i^+$, and counter-answer $y_i^-$, hidden activations $h_l(x_i, y_i) = \text{LayerOutput}_l(x_i \oplus y_i)[:, -1, :]$ at Layer~8 (0-indexed decoder block \texttt{model.model.layers[8]}, the 9th of 28 blocks in Qwen2.5-7B) are collected via forward hook at the final token position of unpadded forward passes ($\text{batch\_size}=1$), ensuring extraction of the true sequence end token without padding artifacts. Prompts $x_i$ use the standardized extraction template (\vn{Dựa vào ngữ cảnh y học sau đây, hãy trả lời câu hỏi:\textbackslash nNgữ cảnh: \{context\}\textbackslash nCâu hỏi: \{question\}\textbackslash nTrả lời: }). The factual steering vector $v_{\text{steer}}^{(l)}$ is computed on $N_{\text{train}}=10,272$ pairs as normalized mean difference:
\begin{equation}
v_{\text{steer}}^{(l)} = \frac{\mu_l^+ - \mu_l^-}{\|\mu_l^+ - \mu_l^-\|_2}, \quad \text{where } \mu_l^\pm = \frac{1}{N_{\text{train}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{train}}} h_l(x_i, y_i^\pm)
\end{equation}
Because hidden states are extracted at the sequence-terminal token position ($[:, -1, :]$), the resulting difference vector $v_{\text{steer}}$ may capture confounding superficial attributes alongside factual semantics—such as terminal punctuation, sequence length priors, EOS prediction tendencies, or expository phrasing style. To evaluate directional selectivity and rule out unoriented artifacts, we evaluate four baseline control conditions: (i)~\textbf{Negative Steering:} Inverting the steering direction along the anti-reference vector (implemented as $\alpha_0=-18.0$ with $+v_{\text{steer}}$, or equivalently $\alpha_0=+18.0$ with $-v_{\text{steer}}$, injecting $-18.0 \cdot v_{\text{steer}}$ to avoid double-negative ambiguity, RefPref 62.80\%). (ii)~\textbf{Random Label Permutation ($v_{\text{shuf}}$):} Swapping labels ($y_i^+ \leftrightarrow y_i^-$) across $N_{\text{flipped}}=5,198 / 10,272$ pairs (50.60\%, RefPref 68.60\%). (iii)~\textbf{Covariance-Matched Distribution ($N=20$):} Vectors $v_{\text{cov}}^{(r)} = \frac{L z_r}{\|L z_r\|_2}$ sampled via Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage matrix factorization $\Sigma_{\text{data}} = L L^T$ from residual difference vectors $d_i = h_i^+ - h_i^-$ on the $N_{\text{train}}=10,272$ pool ($74.81\% \pm 0.95\%$). (iv)~\textbf{Isotropic Gaussian Distribution ($N=100$):} $v_{\text{rand}}^{(r)} = z_r / \|z_r\|_2$ for $z_r \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_{3584})$ (seeds 1 to 100, $55.82\% \pm 4.15\%$), establishing a standardized descriptive separation of $z_{\text{sep}}=5.20$ sample standard deviations above the random direction placebo mean: $z_{\text{sep}} = \frac{77.40\% - 55.82\%}{4.15\%} = 5.20$, reporting descriptive empirical rank $1/101$ ($p_{\text{emp}} = 0.0099$) within the tested 100-vector isotropic pool rather than a formal hypothesis test of domain-general factual semantics. We note that $N=20$ covariance samples and a single permutation split represent bounded preliminary baselines rather than exhaustive proofs of unique factual semantics.

\subsection{Early-Stopping Steering Taxonomy \& Activation-Norm Dynamics}\label{sec:taxonomy}
Residual stream activations $h_l^{(t)}$ at Layer~8 are modified during autoregressive generation ($t \ge 1$) according to:
\begin{equation}
\hat{h}_l^{(t)} = h_l^{(t)} + \alpha(t) \cdot v_{\text{steer}}^{(l)}
\end{equation}
We evaluate three distinct temporal intervention schedules under fixed initial scale $\alpha_0$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\text{decay}}(t) = \begin{cases} \alpha_0 \left(1 - \frac{t-1}{K}\right), & 1 \le t \le K \\ 0, & t > K \end{cases}, \quad
\alpha_{\text{cutoff}}(t) = \begin{cases} \alpha_0, & 1 \le t \le K \\ 0, & t > K \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Continuous Steering ($K=\infty$) applies $\alpha(t) = \alpha_0$ for all $t \ge 1$. Unsteered prefill ($t=0$) predicts $y_1$; the model executes $L_i = T_{\text{gen},i} - 1$ decoding steps ($t \in [1, L_i]$, influencing $y_2 \ldots y_{T_{\text{gen},i}}$). Cumulative executed dose scales as $D_{\text{cutoff}} = m \cdot |\alpha_0|$ and $D_{\text{decay}} = |\alpha_0|\left[ m - \frac{m(m-1)}{2K} \right]$ with $m = \min(K, \max(0, L_i))$, reaching $D_{\text{decay}} = 8.5 |\alpha_0|$ for $L_i \ge 16$. Phase activation norms (baseline active phase mean $\bar{H}_{8,\text{active}}=52.88$, post-16 phase mean $\bar{H}_{8,\text{post16}}=52.20$) are evaluated across active ($t \in [1, \min(L_i, 16)]$) and post-16 ($t \in [17, L_i]$) phases. Prompt-level mean norms $\bar{h}_{8,i}^{\text{phase}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{i,\text{phase}}|} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{i,\text{phase}}} \|h_8^{(i)}(t)\|_2$ are averaged across sequences as $\bar{H}_{8,\text{phase}} = \frac{1}{N_{\text{phase}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{phase}}} \bar{h}_{8,i}^{\text{phase}}$, excluding sequences terminating early ($L_i \le 16$) from the post-16 denominator. Nominal Matched Cumulative Dose Control sets per-step coefficient $\alpha_{\text{match}} = \frac{8.5}{200}\alpha_0 = 0.0425 \alpha_0$ ($0.7650$ for $\alpha_0=18.0$), calibrated to match Linear Decay's dose over a nominal 200-token horizon ($L_{\text{max}}=199$ post-prefill steps executing $199 \times 0.0425\alpha_0 = 8.4575\alpha_0$, with early EOS scaling executed dose with $L_i$).

\textbf{BERTScore Reference-Preference Criterion \& Repetition Metric.} Factual preference alignment is evaluated via BERTScore \cite{ref15} comparisons against reference $y^+$ and counter-answer $y^-$: $\text{RefPref}(\hat{y}) = \mathbb{1}[\text{BS}(\hat{y}, y^+) > \text{BS}(\hat{y}, y^-)]$, where $\text{BS}(\cdot,\cdot)$ denotes BERTScore F1 using multilingual BERT (\texttt{bert-base-multilingual-cased}, layer 9, default rescaling/IDF disabled). Importantly, RefPref provides an automated semantic preference proxy rather than clinician-certified correctness; embedding metrics do not perform rule-based symbolic verification of numerical drug dosages, unit accuracy (e.g., mg vs $\mu\text{g}$), clinical contraindications, or drug-drug interactions. Evaluator token pre-check confirmed that under bounded testing ($T=200$), completions ($\hat{y}$, max 178 mBERT subwords) and reference texts ($y^+, y^-$, max 153 mBERT subwords) remained strictly within mBERT's 512-subword evaluator window (0\% truncation). Under natural completion ($T=800$), completions average 325.6 Qwen subwords (~276--299 mBERT subwords, max 581--926 mBERT subwords across conditions), with only 1.2\%--2.2\% of completions exceeding 512 subwords and undergoing standard tail truncation. Text repetition is measured via word-level 4-gram repetition (\textbf{Rep-4}):
\begin{equation}
\text{Rep-4}(\hat{y}_i) = \begin{cases} 100 \times \left(1 - \frac{|\text{unique 4-grams in } G_4(\hat{y}_i)|}{|\text{total 4-grams in } G_4(\hat{y}_i)|}\right), & |G_4(\hat{y}_i)| > 0 \\ 0.0\%, & |G_4(\hat{y}_i)| = 0 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $G_4(\hat{y}_i)$ denotes consecutive word 4-grams extracted via whitespace tokenization, aggregated as sample macro-average across $N=500$ sequences.

\textbf{Experimental Setup \& Implementation Protocol.} All experiments evaluate Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \cite{ref4} in 4-bit NormalFloat4 (NF4) quantization via BitsAndBytes 0.42.0 on NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs (CUDA 12.1, PyTorch 2.2.0, Transformers 4.38.2). Generation runs use deterministic greedy decoding (sampling temperature $\tau=0.0$, top-$p=1.0$, seed 42) with key-value caching enabled, terminating upon emission of stop tokens (\texttt{<|im\_end|>}, ID 151645; \texttt{<|endoftext|>}, ID 151643) or reaching the maximum token budget; EOS Hit Rate tracks sequences terminating via stop tokens prior to exhaustion. ChatML system prompt: ``\textit{\vn{Bạn là một chuyên gia y dược lâm sàng Việt Nam. Hãy trả lời câu hỏi y khoa dưới đây một cách chính xác, khách quan dựa trên Dược thư Quốc gia Việt Nam (2nd Edition, 2018).}}''. Generation executes with batch size 1 to prevent padding artifacts. The PyTorch hook on Layer~8 is registered before generation and removed immediately after completion; manifests record stop token IDs and cumulative injected dose magnitude $D = \sum_{t=1}^{L_i} |\alpha(t)|$. Prefill ($t=0$) resets the step counter, while single-token decoding steps increment $t$:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python,basicstyle=\ttfamily\scriptsize,aboveskip=2pt,belowskip=2pt,columns=flexible]
def hook_fn(module, inp, out):
  h = out[0] if isinstance(out, tuple) else out
  if h.shape[1] > 1: self.t = 0; return out  # reset on prefill
  self.t += 1
  w = max(0.0, 1.0 - (self.t-1)/self.K) if self.sched == "linear_decay" \
      else (1.0 if self.t <= self.K else 0.0) if self.sched == "hard_cutoff" \
      else (1.0 if self.sched == "continuous" else 0.0)
  if self.alpha_0 * w != 0.0:
    h[:, -1, :] += (self.alpha_0 * w) * self.v_steer.to(device=h.device, dtype=h.dtype)
  return out
\end{lstlisting}

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) uses 14,576 passages ($180 \pm 45$ words / $215 \pm 52$ subwords) from the drug formulary \cite{ref6}, evaluating Lexical BM25 (PyVi tokenizer, $k_1=1.5, b=0.75$), Dense Retrieval (BGE-M3, 1024-d cosine similarity), and Hybrid RRF ($k_{\text{rrf}}=60$). Top-1 passage ($k=1$) is formatted into the prompt context. Retrieval quality measures Mean Reciprocal Rank over $|Q|=500$ queries: $\text{MRR}@10 = \frac{1}{|Q|} \sum_{i=1}^{|Q|} \text{RR}_{10}(q_i)$ with $\text{RR}_{10}(q_i) = 1/r_i$ for ground-truth monograph rank $r_i \le 10$ ($0$ otherwise). Hyperparameter selection (layer $l=8$, cutoff $K=16$, scale $\alpha_0=18.0$) was established via validation grid search across $N_{\text{val}}=2,223$, disjoint from final test set $N_{\text{test}}=500$. Code and artifacts are available at \url{https://github.com/dothanhtuan1704-droid/early-stopping-steering}.

Under high-cap natural completion (Panel B, $T=800$), models maintain high EOS Hit rates (99.80\%--100.00\%): Hard Cutoff ($K{=}16$) achieves peak observed accuracy at \textbf{70.60\% RefPref} (+5.20~pp over baseline 65.40\%, item-level exact McNemar $p=0.00086$, Holm $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0026$, 95\% CI $[+2.40, +8.20]$~pp), whereas Linear Decay yields 67.80\% and continuous steering yields 68.60\% ($p=0.0365$, Holm $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0730$). Notably, while pairwise RefPref preference increases by +5.20~pp, absolute BERTScore F1 against reference answers $y^+$ slightly decreases (0.6695 vs 0.6779) alongside a modest rise in Rep-4 (5.35\% vs 4.10\%). We hypothesize that this divergence may be related to output length dynamics (mean 325.6 vs 248.7 Qwen subwords); however, because token-level Precision and Recall were not separately logged and no length-controlled regression was conducted, attributing the F1 drop to output length remains an unverified hypothesis rather than an empirically established mechanism. Thus, RefPref gains do not imply uniform token-level convergence toward the reference string.

\subsection{Empirical Activation-Norm Dynamics}\label{sec:norm_dynamics}
To characterize the activation mechanics of early-stopping steering at Layer~8, we evaluate activation norms across $N=50$ diagnostic prompts under greedy decoding ($T=100$):

\textbf{1.~Within-Run Norm Offset:} Let $\mathcal{P}_t = \{i \mid L_i \ge t\}$ denote active prompts executing decoding step $t \ge 1$, with $N_t = |\mathcal{P}_t|$. Single-step mean pre-hook hidden norm across active prompts is $S_8^{(t)} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_t|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t} \|h_8^{(t)}(x_i)\|_2$. Within-run norm offset is $\Delta \text{norm}(t) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}_t|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{P}_t} ( \|\hat{h}_8^{(t)}(x_i)\|_2 - \|h_8^{(t)}(x_i)\|_2 )$, where $\hat{h}_8^{(t)} = h_8^{(t)} + \alpha(t) v_{\text{steer}}$. Under unsteered baseline ($\alpha(t)=0$), unmodified whole-phase mean magnitude is $\bar{H}_{8,\text{active}} = 52.88$ ($t \in [1, 16]$) and $\bar{H}_{8,\text{post16}} = 52.20$ ($t \in [17, L_i]$). Continuous steering ($K=\infty$) induces persistent elevation ($\Delta_{\text{norm}} = +1.90$ active, $+2.51$ post-16). Step 20 injection under extended schedules yields corresponding offsets ($\Delta_{\text{norm}}^{(20)}=+1.12$ for $K=24, \alpha=3.75$; $+1.98$ for $K=32, \alpha=7.31$). Post-cutoff ($t > K$), $\alpha(t)=0$ yields $\Delta_{\text{norm}} = 0.00$ by construction (as direct vector injection stops by definition), while downstream prefix conditioning persists in the KV-cache.

\textbf{2.~Independent Cross-Condition Post-Cutoff Norm Comparison:} Evaluating prompt-level mean pre-hook norms under Hard Cutoff against independent unsteered baseline completions across $N=50$ paired prompt completions over post-cutoff steps $t \in [17, L_i]$ reveals prompt-level post-cutoff pre-hook norms under Hard Cutoff ($51.84$) exhibit no statistically significant difference relative to baseline ($52.20$, mean prompt-level difference $-0.36$, sample SD $= 1.54$, paired $t$-test $p = 0.1050 > 0.05$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.80, +0.08]$). While $p > 0.05$ fails to reject equal mean scalar norm magnitudes rather than establishing dynamic trajectory equivalence or proving hidden states are unaltered, the lack of significant scalar divergence ($51.84$ vs $52.20$) shows that early intervention avoids runaway norm growth, while ongoing performance gain is consistent with an autoregressive prefix conditioning hypothesis.

\subsection{Directional Controls, Subspace Selectivity \& RAG Comparisons}\label{sec:baselines_rag}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} reports performance across controls and multi-retriever RAG. Negative steering reduces RefPref to 62.80\% ($-10.40$~pp vs baseline). Isotropic controls ($N=100$, $55.82\% \pm 4.15\%$ sample SD) establish global directional selectivity ($z_{\text{sep}}=5.20\sigma$, descriptive empirical rank $1/101$, $p_{\text{emp}} = 0.0099$). Label-shuffled steering ($v_{\text{shuf}}$, 68.60\%) indicates that contrastive pair semantics are essential. Furthermore, covariance-matched vectors ($N=20$, $74.81\% \pm 0.95\%$ sample SD) achieve +1.61~pp over baseline despite near-zero mean cosine similarity with $v_{\text{steer}}$ ($-0.06$). Because zero-mean Gaussian covariance sampling is centrally symmetric ($v \leftrightarrow -v$), sampling from $\Sigma_{\text{data}}$ restricts perturbations to the empirical subspace of contrastive data variance (avoiding isotropic collapse), while the oriented centroid $+v_{\text{steer}}$ ($77.40\%$) provides directed optimization (+2.59~pp / $2.73\sigma$ above the covariance mean), though $N=20$ samples provide a bounded preliminary baseline rather than an exhaustive manifold proof.

\subsection{Temporal Shift, Prefix Disentanglement \& Combined RAG Evaluation}\label{sec:advanced_analysis}
\textbf{Temporal Window Injection Shift Control.} To evaluate whether intervention timing influences factual alignment independently of duration, we compare equal 16-step injection windows under Hard Cutoff ($\alpha_0=18.0$, Layer~8) across matched horizons. Under the controlled 200-token horizon ($T=200$), factual alignment exhibits monotonic attenuation as injection is deferred: Early intervention ($t \in [1, 16]$) achieves 77.00\% RefPref (+3.80~pp over baseline 73.20\%, item-level exact McNemar $p=0.0110$), Delayed injection ($t \in [17, 32]$) yields 73.40\% (+0.20~pp, 367/500), and Later injection ($t \in [33, 48]$) drops to 72.80\% (364/500; net $-2$ items vs baseline 73.20\%, paired counts $b=16, c=14$, item-level exact McNemar $p=0.8555$). Extended completion ($T=800$) evaluated deferred windows for repetition tracking (5.25\%--5.56\% Rep-4 vs 4.10\% baseline). Because generation terminates dynamically upon stop token emission, delayed windows require completions to sustain sufficient length ($L_i \ge 32$ and $L_i \ge 48$) to receive their full nominal 16-step dose. While clinical explanations generally exceed 50 tokens (mean $\sim 170$ tokens at $T=200$, mean $\sim 295$ tokens at $T=800$), completions terminating earlier suffer from dose truncation in delayed windows. Consequently, this monotonic decline (77.00\% $\rightarrow$ 73.40\% $\rightarrow$ 72.80\%) serves strictly as an observational ablation showing attenuated sensitivity when steering is deferred, rather than a proven pairwise-differentiable effect.

\section{Discussion \& Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}
\textbf{Factual Preference vs Clinical Medical Correctness.} Automated BERTScore RefPref evaluates semantic similarity against reference answers, functioning as a preference proxy rather than a clinician-certified metric of medical correctness. Specifically, embedding metrics do not perform rule-based symbolic verification of numerical drug dosages, unit accuracy (e.g., mg vs $\mu\text{g}$), clinical contraindications, or drug-drug interactions. Generated model outputs were not subjected to blinded adjudication by practicing physicians or clinical pharmacists; human auditing was restricted to an $N=50$ formulary-grounding spot check on benchmark source pairs (94.0\% alignment, 47/50, 95\% Clopper-Pearson CI $[83.5\%, 98.7\%]$), confirming that ViHaluEval-Medical is a rule-curated benchmark rather than an exhaustively certified clinical ground truth. Additionally, candidate pairs were synthesized using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-AWQ, while the evaluated model belongs to the same family (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct). Although reference preference uses an external encoder (mBERT), this shared architecture introduces potential same-family inductive bias; cross-model generalization (e.g., Llama-3, Gemma-2) remains an essential future test.

\textbf{BERTScore F1 Divergence \& Output Length.} Under natural completion ($T=800$), pairwise RefPref increases by +5.20~pp while absolute BERTScore F1 against reference answers slightly decreases (0.6695 vs 0.6779) alongside a modest rise in Rep-4 (5.35\% vs 4.10\%). We hypothesize that this divergence relates to output length dynamics (mean 325.6 vs 248.7 Qwen subwords), lowering token-level precision against concise monograph snippets even as relative similarity favors the steered output over counter-answers. However, because token-level Precision and Recall were not separately logged and no length-controlled regression was conducted, attributing the F1 drop to output length remains an unverified hypothesis. RefPref gains do not signify uniform token-level convergence toward the reference string.

\textbf{Intervention Timing vs Cumulative Dose Confounding.} Comparing schedules under matched initial scale $\alpha_0=18.0$ is intrinsically confounded by cumulative intervention dose ($16|\alpha_0|=288$ for Cutoff vs $8.5|\alpha_0|=153$ for Decay vs $L_i|\alpha_0|$, reaching up to $199 \times 18 = 3,582$ for a completion that executes all 199 post-prefill decoding steps). Because intervention duration and cumulative dose co-vary across schedules, current evaluations reflect their joint operational effect; isolating the independent causal role of early timing from cumulative dose requires fine-grained dose-matched ablations across varying horizons. While Nominal Matched Dose ($0.0425\alpha_0$, executing $8.4575\alpha_0$ over $L_{\text{max}}=199$ post-prefill steps) calibrates dose-rate to the nominal 200-token horizon, early EOS emission makes executed dose variable across instances; hence, degradation under Continuous steering cannot be causally isolated from cumulative overdose.

\textbf{Activation Norm Mechanics \& Cluster-Adjusted Uncertainty.} Post-cutoff ($t > K$), within-run offset $\Delta_{\text{norm}} = 0.00$ occurs by design ($\alpha(t)=0$). The cross-condition comparison against unsteered inference ($51.84$ vs $52.20$, paired $t$-test $p=0.1050 > 0.05$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.80, +0.08]$) fails to reject equal mean scalar norms, indicating that mean scalar magnitudes do not significantly diverge from baseline; however, this lack of statistical separation does not prove dynamic trajectory equivalence or rule out subtle representation shifts. Test questions may cluster by active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) or drug monograph. While primary tests treat items as item-level observations, intra-cluster correlation across questions sharing a monograph means effective sample size may be smaller than nominal $N=500$, potentially rendering standard item-level McNemar and bootstrap confidence intervals anti-conservative (overly optimistic); cluster-robust variance estimation remains an important direction. Additionally, Holm step-down adjustments were applied locally within individual evaluation panels, rather than globally across all exploratory comparisons.

\textbf{Directional Controls \& Deployment Boundaries.} Random label permutation ($N_{\text{flipped}}=5,198/10,272$, 68.60\%) supports the importance of contrastive semantics. Covariance-matched directions ($N=20$, $74.81\% \pm 0.95\%$ sample SD) achieve +1.61~pp over baseline despite near-zero mean cosine similarity with $v_{\text{steer}}$ ($-0.06$). Because zero-mean Gaussian covariance sampling is centrally symmetric ($v \leftrightarrow -v$), sampling from $\Sigma_{\text{data}}$ restricts perturbations to empirical contrastive variance (avoiding isotropic collapse, 55.82\%), while $+v_{\text{steer}}$ supplies directed centroid alignment (+2.59~pp / $2.73\sigma$). We acknowledge that $N=20$ samples and a single permutation split represent computationally bounded control benchmarks, insufficient to conclusively prove that $v_{\text{steer}}$ isolates an exclusive factual semantic direction. Because vector extraction occurs at sequence-terminal tokens, $v_{\text{steer}}$ may encode stylistic, length, or punctuation patterns alongside factuality. Deployment claims should strictly reflect measured quantities: early-stopping steering provides a lightweight, retrieval-free preference alignment mechanism eliminating external retrieval indexing (142.5~ms) and context prompt expansion with an observed runtime difference of +0.01~s vs baseline (unresolved within run-to-run measurement variability $\pm 0.30$~s), without asserting unmeasured end-to-end clinical deployment guarantees.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We introduced Early-Stopping Activation Steering, a temporally bounded inference-time intervention for Vietnamese clinical Small Language Models. Evaluated on ViHaluEval-Medical (14,674 core pairs screened from 14,700 candidates derived from the Vietnamese National Drug Formulary, 2nd Edition, 2018), restricting steering to initial $K=16$ decoding steps yields reference-preference alignment with zero model updates. Under natural completion ($T=800$), Hard Cutoff ($K=16$) achieves 70.60\% RefPref (+5.20~pp, item-level exact McNemar $p=0.00086$, Holm $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0026$). Under bounded stress testing ($T=200$), Linear Decay ($K=16$) achieves 77.40\% (+4.20~pp, item-level exact McNemar $p=0.0055$, Holm $p_{\text{adj}}=0.0165$), yielding no statistically significant difference from Hybrid RRF RAG (76.20\%, $p=0.4709$) and significantly outperforming lexical BM25 (+8.80~pp, $p=3.85 \times 10^{-7}$) without external retrieval overhead and with minimal runtime difference (+0.01~s vs baseline, within $\pm 0.30$~s measurement variability). While exploratory combination of Hybrid RAG with Hard Cutoff yields 80.40\% (+1.00~pp, $p=0.3018$, non-significant marginal gain), early-stopping steering provides a lightweight, retrieval-free preference alignment mechanism warranting clinician-adjudicated validation.
