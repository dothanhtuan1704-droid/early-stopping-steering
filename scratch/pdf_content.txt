Abstract. Small Language Models (SLMs) offer potential for privacy-
preserving clinical decision support but frequently exhibit domain-specific
hallucinations in resource-scarce languages like Vietnamese—such as
miscalculating pediatric dosages or violating pregnancy contraindications.
We propose Early-Stopping Activation Steering, a framework evaluating
temporallyboundedactivationsteeringonQwen2.5-7B-InstructatLayer8
with Hard Cutoff and Linear Decay schedules restricting vector injection
to the firstK=16decoding steps (t∈ [1, 16]). Under high-cap natural
completion (max_new_tokens=800), Hard Cutoff (K=16) achieves 70.60%
reference-preference accuracy (+5.20 pp over baseline, Holm-adjusted
padj = 0.0026) with 100% EOS hit rate. Under bounded-generation stress
testing (max_new_tokens=200), Linear Decay steering (K=16) increases
accuracy from 73.00% to 77.20% (+4.20 pp, McNemarp = 0.0055),
outperforming non-oracle BM25 RAG by +8.60 pp with zero context
overhead. Combining Steering with Hybrid RAG achieves peak 80.40%
RefPref accuracy (+1.00 pp over RAG alone, +15.00 pp over baseline)
while suppressing 4-gram repetition to 4.46%. Descriptive placebo controls
(N=100random vectors,55 .82%±4.15%) establish a+5.15σ separation
above isotropic random direction perturbation.

1 Introduction
Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across healthcare
tasks [3]. However, in specialized domains requiring high factual precision—such
as clinical pharmacology and prescription guidance in non-English languages—
standard models suffer from domain-specific hallucinations [7]. In Vietnamese
clinical settings, these hallucinations range from benign stylistic errors to danger-
ous medical contradictions, such as recommending contraindicated medications
during pregnancy or miscalculating weight-based pediatric dosages.
Traditional mitigation strategies rely on post-hoc supervised fine-tuning (SFT)
[10] or Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) [5]. Although RAG provides

external knowledge contexts, models may ignore retrieved evidence when internal
activation pathways favor non-truthful generation [11], additionally incurring
heavy context window overheads and retrieval latencies. Supervised fine-tuning
demands substantial computational resources and risks catastrophic forgetting of
general abilities [12].
Recently, Representation Engineering (RepE) [1] and Inference-Time Inter-
vention (ITI) [2] have emerged as lightweight, non-destructive alternatives. By
extracting a truthfulness steering vectorvsteer from hidden activation contrasts
and injecting it into intermediate layers during generation, activation steering
shifts model outputs toward factual accuracy without modifying underlying
weights. Related approaches such as Activation Addition (ActAdd) [8] and con-
trastive activation addition [9] have demonstrated the viability of linear steering
for behavioral control.
However, continuous activation steering (K = ∞) induces persistent hidden-
state norm elevation over long output sequences, associated with cumulative
activation displacement and text repetition. In this paper, we presentEarly-
Stopping Activation Steering, evaluating temporally bounded intervention
via Hard Cutoff and Linear Decay schedules restricting vector injection to the
firstK= 16decoding steps (t∈[1,16], influencing tokensy 2 . . . y17).

2 Related Work
Hallucination in Clinical LMs.Ji et al. [7] survey hallucination phenomena,
categorizing them into intrinsic and extrinsic types. In medical settings, Singhal

et al. [3] showed that while large models encode clinical knowledge, they remain
prone to factual errors in drug interactions and contraindications. The HaluE-
val benchmark [13] and TruthfulQA [16] established evaluation protocols that
informed our domain-specific ViHaluEval-Medical benchmark.
Inference-Time Intervention & Representation Engineering.Rather
than modifying model weights through parameter-efficient fine-tuning [ 10],
inference-time methods intervene directly in internal representations. Li et
al. [2] proposed ITI, identifying truthful directions in attention heads. Zou
et al. [1] generalized this to Representation Engineering (RepE). Turner et
al. [8] introduced ActAdd, and Rimsky et al. [9] proposed contrastive activation
addition. Our work addresses continuous steering limitations by comparing Hard
Cutoff and Linear Decay schedules.
Evaluation Metrics.Traditional n-gram overlap metrics like ROUGE [14]
fail to capture semantic equivalence in multilingual clinical settings. Zhang et
al. [15] proposed BERTScore, which computes token-level cosine similarities using
contextual BERT embeddings. We adopt a BERTScore-based reference-preference
criterion as a proxy for factual preference alignment.
3 Methodology
3.1 ViHaluEval-Medical Benchmark Construction
The ViHaluEval-Medical benchmark comprises 14,674 expert-structured Viet-
namese clinical Q&A pairs derived from the official Vietnamese National Drug
Formulary (Dược thư Quốc gia Việt Nam, 2nd Edition, 2018) [6] (14.1M char-
acters across 1,026 monographs). A vLLM engine powered by Qwen2.5-7B-
Instruct-AWQ generated distinct clinical question records (qi), each paired with
a pharmacist-verified reference answer (y+
i ) and a hallucinated counter-answer
y−
i (hallucinated_answer). The dataset spans three clinical categories: Special
Dosage Modifications (N = 5, 000, 34.08%), Contradictory Pregnancy Safety
(N= 4,885, 33.29%), and Misleading Drug Interactions (N= 4,789, 32.64%).
Manual adjudication by 3 licensed clinical pharmacists achieved high inter-
annotator agreement (mean pairwise Cohen’sκ = 0.91; Fleiss’κ = 0.89). Bench-
mark data is partitioned at the active pharmaceutical ingredient (API) level into
a development split (Ndev = 12, 495, 85.15%: 70.0% trainingNtrain = 10, 272for
activation mean estimation, 15.15% validationNval = 2, 223for hyperparameter
tuning) and a held-out test evaluation subset (Ntest = 500, seed 42): Pregnancy
Safety (N = 165), Drug Interactions (N = 160), and Special Dosage (N = 175).

class SteeringHookHandler:
def __init__(self, v_steer, alpha_0=18.0, K=16, schedule="linear_decay"):
self.v_steer, self.alpha_0, self.K, self.schedule, self.t = v_steer, alpha_0, K,
schedule, 0
def reset(self): self.t = 0
def hook_fn(self, module, input, output):
out = output[0] if isinstance(output, tuple) else output
if out.shape[1] > 1: self.t = 0; return output
self.t += 1
a_t = self.alpha_0 * max(0.0, 1.0 - (self.t-1)/self.K) if self.schedule == "
linear_decay" else (self.alpha_0 if self.t <= self.K else 0.0) if self.schedule
== "hard_cutoff" else (self.alpha_0 if self.schedule == "continuous" else 0.0)
if a_t != 0.0: out[:, -1, :] += a_t * self.v_steer.to(device=out.device, dtype=out.
dtype)
return output

5 Discussion & Limitations
Reference-preference accuracy is a semantic proxy for factual alignment rather
than a clinician-adjudicated correctness rate; validation against blinded clinician
labels is required in future work. Equal initialα0 produces unequal cumulative

6 Conclusion
We introduced Early-Stopping Activation Steering, a temporally bounded
inference-time intervention for Vietnamese clinical Small Language Models.
Evaluated on ViHaluEval-Medical (14,674 records derived from the Vietnamese
National Drug Formulary, 2nd Edition, 2018), restricting steering to initial
K = 16decoding steps yields reference-preference alignment with zero
model-weight updates and zero retrieval latency. Under natural completion
(max_new_tokens=800), Hard Cutoff ( K = 16) achieves 70.60% RefPref
accuracy (+5.20 pp, exact McNemar p = 0.00086, padj = 0.0026). Under
bounded stress testing (max_new_tokens=200), Linear Decay (K = 16) achieves
77.20% (+4.20 pp,p = 0.0055). Compared to non-oracle BM25 RAG (68.60%),
early-stopping steering provides +8.60 pp superior accuracy (p = 6.11 × 10−7),
offering a practical alignment mechanism warranting clinician-adjudicated
validation.
